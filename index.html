Main Workout

After the warm-up — what to actually climb, for the selected session type
Session-specific

Limit bouldering / coordination — technique-first at your current grade

  • 3–6 problems at or near today's session max (same reference point as your Phase 1 ladder — shift with your actual daily max, not a fixed V6).
  • Before each attempt: call your beta out loud — footwork sequence, hip position (flag or backstep), where you'll pause vs. move continuously. At V5, the limiter is usually precision and positioning, not force — treat each try as a technique rep that happens to be hard, not a power rep.
  • 1–2 attempts per problem, full recovery between every single try: rest 3–5 min regardless of success or failure.
  • On steep terrain specifically: prioritize problems that force a static, controlled position over a problem's crux rather than ones solved by a single dynamic move — that's the gap you're training.
  • Stop the session once 2 consecutive attempts feel noticeably worse than earlier ones — that's neural fatigue outrunning recovery. Pushing past this trains sloppy movement, not power.
  • Total hard tries: 6–10, done. This is a quality day, not a volume day.

Pick one — given your current V4 plateau, default to Option B most weeks

  • Option A — ARC (capacity): 20–30+ min continuous easy climbing (auto belay, top-rope laps, or easy circuits). Stay under ~3–4/10 pump the whole time — if you're breathing hard or gripping tight, back off the terrain. Builds aerobic base, not skill.
  • Option B — Technique / slab drilling (recommended right now): problems 1–2 grades below max, slab or vertical terrain, with one constraint — zero dynamic moves. Find the static, controlled version of every move. Place feet precisely with no re-adjusting once weighted. Use flagging/backstepping to keep hips over your base instead of squaring up and muscling through. Repeat each problem 2–3 times for clean execution, not speed.
This is exactly the fix for what you flagged: relying on dynamism was masking footwork and static-position gaps. Steep terrain let you cheat around it; slab and controlled climbing won't. Option B is where that gets rebuilt.

Lead climbing — sustained pump & route-reading

  • One easy lead or top-rope lap first — well below your pump ladder's working grade, pure movement rehearsal. This isn't for finger priming (the ladder already did that on boulders); it's to re-acclimate to clipping, rope weight, and positioning before you're on a route that actually matters.
  • 3–5 routes at a grade that produces steady, climbable pump — hard enough to demand real forearm management, not so hard you're falling from pure difficulty.
  • Read the route from the ground first where possible — plan rest positions and clip stances before you leave the ground, not mid-route when you're already pumping.
  • On-sight or flash attempts count double for this stimulus — redpointing a known route trains memory and efficiency, but on-sight route-reading under pump is the harder skill you're after.
  • Prioritize routes with sustained steep sections over routes that are just one hard crux move — that's the same steep-wall endurance gap the Lactic 4x4s are built around.
  • Shake out deliberately at any no-hands or low-grip rest — this is a skill, not wasted time, and it's exactly what separates good lead climbers from strong boulderers on routes.
  • Total volume: 3–5 routes, done. Stop once footwork and clipping start getting sloppy from fatigue — that's the same "neural fatigue" cutoff as your other sessions, just route-shaped.

  • Zone 2 target: 3–4 sessions/week, 30–45 min each (~150+ min/week total) — this is the volume threshold most associated with HDL/triglyceride improvement, more than any single long session.
  • Intensity cue: conversational pace — you should be able to hold a conversation without gasping. If you don't have HR zones dialed in yet, "could keep going for an hour" is a good gut-check that you're not drifting into Zone 3.
  • Placement logic: Zone 2 sits on the days between Power/Lactic sessions — low intensity enough not to interfere with tendon recovery, and it uses the recovery day productively instead of being fully sedentary.
  • Steep-wall focus: on Power and Lead days, bias route/boulder selection toward overhanging terrain specifically — that's the goal, so make sure session content reflects it rather than defaulting to whatever's set at your gym.
  • Flex day: Saturday is the easiest day to compress or skip if the week gets busy — it's additive volume, not a day the other sessions depend on.
